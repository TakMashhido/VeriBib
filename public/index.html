Frequently Asked Questions

Learn how VeriBib detects citation hallucinations, audits references, and verifies web publications.

VeriBib cross-references your bibliography in real time against official scholarly registries (Crossref, OpenAlex, arXiv, PubMed) and live web domains. It uses symmetric title similarity, first-author weighting, and DOI resolution to expose AI-hallucinated authors, fake DOIs, or fabricated journal names.

Large Language Models generate text based on statistical token probabilities rather than an indexed scientific database. When asked for references, they often invent plausible-sounding titles, real researcher names, and fake journal volumes or DOIs that do not exist in scientific literature.

Yes. Scholarly registries only index formal academic literature. VeriBib features a live web engine that queries referenced URLs, verifies HTTP 200 reachability, and inspects OpenGraph titles and author tags to validate technical blogs and research posts without false hallucination alerts.

VeriBib supports APA, IEEE, BibTeX, MLA, Chicago, Harvard, Vancouver, raw DOIs, and bare arXiv identifiers (e.g., 1706.03762).

Basic checkers use asymmetric substring matching where short real titles falsely match long fake titles. VeriBib uses symmetric Dice coefficient token matching with stopword filtering and length ratio penalties to ensure high precision.

Yes, VeriBib is 100% free, open-source, and does not require account creation, tracking cookies, or storing your manuscripts on external servers.
