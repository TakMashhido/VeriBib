How it works

Three passes over every reference

  1. 1

    Parse

    Each entry is split out and read into title, authors, year, venue and identifiers. BibTeX braces, LaTeX accents, RIS tags and wrapped lines are handled.

  2. 2

    Cross-check

    DOIs, arXiv IDs, PubMed IDs and ISBNs are resolved directly. Titles are searched in Crossref, OpenAlex, DataCite, arXiv, PubMed and Google Books. Links are opened.

  3. 3

    Score

    The closest record is scored on title, authors, year and venue. Every verdict shows its evidence, so you can see exactly why a reference passed or failed.

What it catches

Built for the ways AI gets citations wrong

Fabricated DOIs

Asks Crossref, DataCite and doi.org directly. A DOI no agency has registered is flagged as fabricated.

Borrowed identifiers

Catches a real DOI or arXiv ID pasted onto a paper it doesn't belong to, which is one of the most common hallucination patterns.

Misattributed papers

Compares the cited authors and year with the record, so a real title credited to the wrong people doesn't pass.

Blogs & web pages

Opens cited URLs, catches dead links and compares the page title and author, so blog citations aren't falsely flagged.

Corrected citations

Copy clean APA, IEEE, MLA or BibTeX built from the registry record, or export a .bib file, Markdown report or CSV.

Private by design

No accounts, cookies or analytics. Only individual lookups reach the public registries, and you can self-host it.

FAQ

Questions, answered

How does VeriBib detect hallucinated citations?

Each reference is parsed into title, authors, year and identifiers, then looked up in Crossref, OpenAlex, DataCite, arXiv, PubMed and Google Books. VeriBib scores how well the closest record matches and flags the patterns typical of fabricated citations: DOIs that do not exist, real DOIs attached to a different paper, real titles credited to the wrong authors, impossible years and dead links.

Why do ChatGPT and other AI tools invent references?

Language models generate text that looks statistically plausible rather than retrieving it from a database. Asked for sources, they often produce convincing titles, real researcher names and well-formed DOIs that do not correspond to any published work.

Which citation formats can I paste?

APA, MLA, Chicago, Harvard, IEEE and Vancouver reference lists, BibTeX and RIS exports, and bare DOIs, arXiv IDs, PubMed IDs, ISBNs or URLs. Mixed lists work too.

Can it check blog posts and web pages?

Yes. When a reference has a URL, VeriBib checks that the link is alive and compares the page title and author with the citation. The self-hosted server does a full page inspection; the static website can only confirm that the host responds, because browsers block reading other sites.

Is my bibliography stored anywhere?

No. There are no accounts, cookies or analytics. Only the individual lookups (titles, author names, identifiers) are sent to the public registries, and your draft is kept only in your own browser.

Does a verified result guarantee a citation is correct?

It means the work exists and its title, authors and year match a registry record. It cannot tell whether the source actually supports the claim you cite it for, so treat VeriBib as a first-pass filter, not a replacement for reading your sources.

Is VeriBib free?

Yes. VeriBib is free and open source under the MIT license. You can use the website or run your own copy with a single Node.js command.
